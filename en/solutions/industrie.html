Spare parts & industry: two characters set two parts apart

A 6204-2RS bearing and a 6204-ZZ bearing share the same number. They are not interchangeable.

The suffix that changes the part

In technical parts, the suffix carries the essential meaning — seal type, pressure class, nominal diameter (the detail of each attribute). A text engine sees a two-character variation and treats the references as equivalent. Your buyer, meanwhile, fits the wrong part on a production machine.

Part number and technical variant

The industrie rule pack activates when you pick this industry, at indexing time. It produces these annotations, each becoming a usable filter:

AnnotationWhat it isolates
RLT_6204the bearing number, series 6000 to 6399
ETANCH_2RSrubber-gasket sealing
ETANCH_ZZsheet-metal shield sealing, ZZ as well as 2Z
PN16the pressure class, PN6 through PN40
DN50the nominal diameter, with or without a space after DN

On top of those: the make — SKF, FAG, NSK, NTN, Timken, INA —, the profile of a V-belt (SPA, SPB, SPC or SPZ, followed by its length) and the size of an O-ring, “40x3” as well as “o-ring 40 x 3.5”. The sealing code then reattaches to the number whatever the order in the label: “6204-2RS” and “2RS 6204” both produce RLT_6204_2RS. That is what makes a supplier nomenclature usable without rewriting it.

Four spellings, one single part

The same part is ordered under four spellings: “6204 2RS”, “6204-2RS”, “6204RS”, “6204 2 RS”. Without structure recognition, each spelling returns a different result — and your catalog looks incomplete when it isn't.

The number separated from the variant

The industry pack separates the number from the sealing variant. You find the exact reference and can knowingly suggest neighboring variants — what a generalist engine merges or misses.
