Hardware & tools: five attributes held in a single reference

An M8x20 screw and an M8x25 screw have nothing in common for your customer. They look nearly identical to a generalist search engine.

Two references, one character apart

Your references encode dimension, material, standard, and head type into one compact string: “M8 x 20 - Stainless A2, DIN 933” (the detail of each attribute). For a text-matching engine, “M8x20” and “M8x25” are nearly identical — two products no one confuses on the shop floor. The error doesn't show up in your search stats: it shows up as a return.

Dimensions, material and standard isolated

The outillage rule pack activates when you pick this industry, at indexing time. It produces these annotations, each becoming a usable filter:

AnnotationWhat it isolates
DIAM_M8the metric diameter, isolated from the rest of the reference
LONG_20the length, whatever the separator: x, ×, * or a hyphen
MAT_INOXthe material, by name or by its A2 and A4 codes
TETE_HEXthe head type, workshop abbreviations included — TH, TF, CHC, BTR
NORME_DIN933the DIN or ISO standard, with or without a space

On top of those: the family — screw, nut, washer, bolt, stud, wall plug — and the strength class, from 4.6 to 12.9. Those six families are recognized in six languages: a Spanish tornillo and a German schraube land in the same filter as the French vis. Two annotations then compose themselves: VIS_M8X20 puts diameter and length back together as one reference, DIAM_M8_INOX crosses diameter with material — the pair a buyer asks for at the counter.

Three spellings, one single product

A customer types “screw m8 20 stainless” with no hyphen or “x”. Another types “M8X20” in solid capitals. A third searches “DIN933” with no space. All three mean the same product — and an unprepared engine returns three different results, or none at all.

Recognized from the first import

The hardware pack recognizes these variants from the very first import, with no configuration on your part. A generalist engine would require you to write and maintain this logic yourself, product line after product line.
