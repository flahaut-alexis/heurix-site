Fashion & retail: a size 38 is not a model number

“Wool turtleneck sweater size 38”: four pieces of information in four different vocabularies. One single query.

Six attributes, no fixed order

Fashion vocabulary mixes product family, cut, material, color, size, and season (the detail of each attribute), often in no fixed order. An engine that treats the phrase as a bag of words doesn't know that “UK 10” is a size, not a reference — or that “turtleneck” is a cut, not a distinct product.

Size, cut and material recognized

The mode rule pack activates when you pick this industry, at indexing time. It produces these annotations, each becoming a usable filter:

AnnotationWhat it isolates
TAILLE_38the size, distinguished from a model number
COL_ROULEthe collar cut, turtleneck or V-neck
MAT_LAINEthe material — merino, cashmere and mohair under one annotation
SAISON_AW25the season in trade format, SS25 as well as AW25 or FW25
JEAN_T38_W30clothing size and jean size reconciled

On top of those: colour, pattern and fastening. Four colour families absorb their shades — burgundy and garnet fall in with red, navy and denim with blue —, four patterns cover the trade's wording (plain, striped, polka dot, floral), and ten synonym groups cover its spellings: t-shirt, tee-shirt, tshirt and “tee shirt” all point at the same aisle, and so do sneaker and trainer. This is the one pack where the difficulty is not reading a code, but accepting that the same garment is written ten different ways.

Three vocabulary gaps, one customer

A customer searches “red sweater size 38”. Your listings say “burgundy”, “size UK 10”, and “knit”. Three vocabulary gaps, and an empty result on a product you have in stock.

Your brand's vocabulary, already known

The fashion pack handles these vocabulary equivalences natively. You add your own — house color names, collection abbreviations — via Custom Rules, with no regex to write.
