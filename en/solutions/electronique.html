Electronics & high-tech: search that understands your references

A 65W charger and a 45W charger look alike as text. Not in use.

One unit mistaken for another

Electronics specs are numeric quantities with units — watts, battery life, volts, gigabytes, ingress protection rating (the detail of each attribute). A text engine treats “65W” as a string: “65W” and “45W” look close to it. For the buyer, one charges their laptop, the other doesn't.

Power, connectors and capacity read

The electronique rule pack activates when you pick this industry, at indexing time. It produces these annotations, each becoming a usable filter:

AnnotationWhat it isolates
PUISSANCE_65Wthe wattage, recognized as a quantity
AUTONOMIE_30Hthe advertised battery life
CONN_USBCthe connector type
NORME_IP68the ingress protection rating
CAPACITE_512GOthe storage capacity

Indexed referenceWhat the engine extracts

HDMI 2.1 cable - 2mCONN_HDMI · FAM_CABLE

Four spellings of one connector

“USB C”, “USB-C”, “Type-C”, and “USBC” all mean the same connector. A customer typing any one of the four forms should find your entire range — not a quarter of it.

A filter by value, not a word

The electronics pack recognizes units and connector spelling variants at indexing time. It also lets you filter by quantity — “chargers above 60W” — something a purely textual index can't do.
