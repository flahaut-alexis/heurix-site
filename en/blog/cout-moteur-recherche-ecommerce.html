How much does an e-commerce search engine cost in 2026?

The listed price doesn't say much until you know what it actually bills. Here's how to compare offers on the market without mixing up units.

A poorly framed question

"How much does a search engine cost?" has no single answer, because billing models vary widely from one provider to another: per search, per indexed product, a flat fee, or some mix of the three. Comparing two offers without understanding what unit is billed almost always leads to a bad estimate.

The market's main models

Algolia bills mainly on search operation volume, with a limited free tier and a cost curve that can surprise past a few tens of thousands of monthly requests. Meilisearch Cloud and Typesense Cloud offer monthly plans starting around twenty euros, generally tied to index size rather than pure search volume. Heurix follows a logic close to that second model: billed on request volume with explicit tiers, starting at €19/month.

The real cost is rarely in the subscription

A search engine's listed price is often only part of the real cost. Initial setup — synonyms, matching rules, relevance sorting — takes developer time, sometimes several days depending on catalog complexity. An engine that ships pre-configured rule packs by industry cuts this hidden cost — not by making it free, but by making a correct result much faster to reach.

Estimate your own volume before comparing prices

The first question isn't "what's the lowest price" but "how much search volume does my site actually generate." A simple estimate: monthly traffic × share of visitors who use search (typically 15–30% on an e-commerce site) × average searches per visitor who searches. The calculator on the pricing page runs this calculation directly from those three figures.

A €19/month engine covering 15,000 requests is plenty for a store just starting out. A "free" engine that bills past a low tier can cost far more once traffic grows.

What to check before signing

Three simple questions avoid a bad surprise: what happens on quota overage — an abrupt cutoff, or transparent billing beyond it? Does the price include setting up matching rules, or is a developer needed on top? And finally — often forgotten — can you test it on a real catalog before paying? A free trial with no credit card remains the most reliable way to confirm an engine fits before committing.
