Cable 3G2.5 or 3x2,5: two notations for a single cross-section

An electrician orders « 3G2.5 ». A colleague writes « 3x2,5 ». The design office notes « 2,5 mm² ». All three name the same cable, and all three coexist in a distributor's catalogue, because they come from different suppliers' imports piled up over a decade. We ran all three through our engine's electrical pack. Only one produces a cross-section annotation — and it is not the one a buyer types.

What the pack reads on a cable, and what it does not

The electrical pack recognises the family from the word in the label — « cable », « câble », « conducteur » and their German and Italian equivalents, a distributor's catalogue usually aggregating European imports. It recognises the harmonised conductor designation, CABLE_h05vv-f for a flexible one, CABLE_h07v-u for a rigid one. It recognises the industrial designation, CABLE_r2v. And it recognises the cross-section — but only when the millimetre is written out.

That is the whole subject. « 3G2.5 » and « 3x2,5 » are the ordering notations: a conductor count, a separator, a cross-section. Neither carries the unit, because an electrician does not say it out loud. The cross-section rule, meanwhile, requires a literal « mm ». So neither ordering notation produces a cross-section annotation, and the quote that writes « 2,5 mm² » is the only one of the three that does.

The measurement

In-house measurement. The labels below were run through the annotation cascade of the electricite pack on 20 October 2026, against the engine at commit d25011d. Each label was run twice: through the Python cascade and through the Rust core that serves indexing and search in production. Both return the same set on all seven labels — an equivalence that is not assumed here, it is re-read on every pass, because only the second path runs at a merchant's.

Product labelAnnotations returned
Cable rigide R2V 2,5 mm2 couronne 100mCABLE_SECT_2,5 · CABLE_r2v · FAM_CABLE · SECTION_2,5
Cable rigide R2V 3G2.5 couronne 100mCABLE_r2v · FAM_CABLE
Cable rigide R2V 3x2,5 couronne 100mCABLE_r2v · FAM_CABLE
Cable souple H05VV-F 3G1.5 blancCABLE_h05vv-f · FAM_CABLE
Cable souple H05Z1-K 3G1.5FAM_CABLE

The first row carries the cross-section, the next two do not. The two ordering notations return exactly the same set of annotations — and it is an agreement from below: they look alike because neither was read, not because the engine worked out that they say the same thing.

What it does to ranking

Same protocol, same day: four cables indexed, one per notation, and a buyer's queries. The scores are the ones the engine returns; they do not depend on the process's iteration order.

A  Cable rigide R2V 3G2.5 couronne 100m
B  Cable rigide R2V 3x2,5 couronne 100m
C  Cable rigide R2V 2,5 mm2 couronne 100m
D  Cable souple H05VV-F 3G1.5 blanc

query « cable 3g2.5 »     ->  A 22.0   B 12.0   C 12.0   D 12.0
query « cable 3x2,5 »     ->  B 22.0   A 12.0   C 12.0   D 12.0
query « cable 2,5 mm2 »   ->  C 38.0   A 12.0   B 12.0   D 12.0
query « cable 3G2,5 »     ->  A 19.6   B 19.6   C 12.0   D 12.0

All four products come back on all four queries: FAM_CABLE is enough to make every one of them a candidate. What separates them is the literal term. « 3G2.5 » puts A first and leaves B tied with a cable of a different cross-section; « 3x2,5 » does the opposite. A buyer who types either notation gets the right cable first — and the other spelling of that same cable at the same rank as an unrelated product.

The fourth row is the instructive one. « cable 3G2,5 » — the ordering notation with the quote's comma — ties A and B exactly, 19.6 each. No rule brought them together: fault tolerance did. That query is one substitution away from « 3g2.5 » and from « 3x2,5 » alike, and the engine says so itself in its result explanation — terme '3g2.5' (depuis '3g2,5', x0.60) on one side, terme '3x2,5' (depuis '3g2,5', x0.60) on the other. The tie is an accident of edit distance, and it only happens for a query sitting one character from both. It is not equivalence: « cable 3g2.5 » and « cable 3x2,5 » do not meet.

Three limits we accept

For the engine, the cross-section only exists if the unit is written. No wider rule fixes that without deciding what « 3G2.5 » means, and the decision is not readable from the text: an electrician reads « 3G2.5 » as « 3 conductors of 2.5 », and « 5G6 » as « 5 conductors of 6 ». A rule taking the number after the G for a cross-section would be right on those two by convention, not by reading. The pack does not carry it, and saying so is more useful than adding it and hoping.

A decimal cross-section can produce a second annotation. On a short label, « Cable R2V 2,5 mm2 » returns SECTION_2,5 and SECTION_5: the comma is a non-word character, it opens a boundary, and the pattern matches a second time on « 5 mm2 ». The spurious value is plausible — 5 mm² is a common cross-section — so it does not stand out. It makes the product show up in a facet where it has no business; it costs no recall.

Two digits followed by « mm » are enough. « Goulotte PVC 40 mm » — a cable trunking — returns SECTION_40, and trunking has no conductor cross-section. The pattern does not bound its context. The two-digit ceiling rules out an enclosure's 500 mm by accident, not by intent.

To which one plain absence is added: the halogen-free series carries a digit in the middle of its designation, and the harmonised-designation pattern does not accept it. « Cable souple H05Z1-K 3G1.5 » returns the family and nothing else. The spellings that actually exist in that series have not been surveyed, so the rule was not widened on a guess.

What to do about it

The fix does not require waiting for an engine release. It requires the cross-section to appear once, in an indexed field — description, attribute, whichever. Measured the same day, on the same commit, with the same label twice and the cross-section carried in the description of the second one:

A   Cable rigide R2V 3G2.5 couronne 100m
A2  Cable rigide R2V 3G2.5 couronne 100m
    description: « Section 2,5 mm2, 3 conducteurs »

query « cable 2,5 mm2 »   ->  A2 30.0   A 12.0
query « cable 3g2.5 »     ->  A  22.0   A2 22.0

A2 is found by both notations, level with A on the ordering one and well ahead of it on the quote's. The cross-section is then read as an annotation, not merely as text: that is what makes it filterable as a facet, and not only searchable.

If your electrical catalogue is already structured by rating, curve, sensitivity and ingress protection, the pack reads them with no configuration — that is the rest of what our Electrical & installation page covers, with the families and attributes it reads. If it carries the cross-section only in ordering notation, one indexed field makes it readable from the first import.
