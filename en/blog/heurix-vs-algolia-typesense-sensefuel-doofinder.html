Heurix vs Algolia, Typesense, Sensefuel, Doofinder: which engine for which need?

Five tools, five different approaches to the same apparent problem — finding the right product fast. Here's where each one factually excels, without trying to pass off a Swiss Army knife as the wrong choice everywhere.

Three families of tools, not one category

Filing these five solutions under a single label would skew the comparison. They answer different needs:

Generalist APIs (Algolia, Typesense) provide raw, fast search infrastructure to configure. They know nothing about your industry out of the box — relevance rules, synonyms, and facets are yours to write.

Plug-and-play solutions (Doofinder, and to a lesser extent Sensefuel) install via a script or module, no developer needed, with a dashboard to drive merchandising — featured products, banners, personalized results.

Specialized engines like Heurix focus on a narrower problem: understanding the structure of a product reference (diameter, size, vintage, ISBN) rather than treating text as a string of words to compare.

Algolia

The industry's best-known name, with infrastructure proven at scale and a very wide integration ecosystem. The free "Build" plan covers small projects (10,000 requests and 1 million records a month); beyond that, usage-based billing on the "Grow" plan runs around $0.50 per 1,000 requests and $0.40 per 1,000 records, with a notably higher rate for the "Grow Plus" plan's AI features. "Premium" and "Elevate" plans move to an annual contract at a negotiated rate. Algolia excels at fast generalist search; understanding that a reference contains a diameter and a length remains a manual configuration job, rule by rule.

Typesense

The most-cited open-source alternative to Algolia: a freely self-hostable engine, with open source code and an active community. Typesense Cloud, the hosted version, bills hourly based on reserved resources (RAM, CPU, storage) rather than a fixed per-tier rate — estimates found online therefore vary widely depending on the chosen configuration, which reflects an infrastructure-based pricing model rather than a genuine disagreement between sources. A good choice for a technical team that wants to keep control of its hosting; reference understanding remains, as with Algolia, something to build yourself.

Sensefuel

A French vendor positioned on e-commerce personalization and merchandising: dashboards built for e-merchandising teams, promoting margin or clearance products, and an AI layer aimed at understanding the natural language of queries. Published pricing starts at €250 a month. Sensefuel explicitly targets marketing/merchandising teams more than developers — a deliberately different posture from an API offering like Heurix or Algolia.

Doofinder

The simplest solution in the group to deploy: a script to install, no developer, live within minutes. Claims over 10,000 customer stores, mainly on Shopify and mainstream e-commerce CMSs. Pricing starts at €35 a month for small catalogs and rises with the number of indexed products. One notable feature, "Custom results," lets you manually associate search terms with products — a pragmatic but manual answer to the problem Heurix solves through automatic structure recognition.

None of these solutions is bad in absolute terms — the question is whether your catalog needs generalist infrastructure to configure, a turnkey merchandising tool, or a fine understanding of technical references from the moment of indexing.

Where Heurix sits

Heurix isn't trying to compete with any of these four solutions on their own turf. It's an API, like Algolia and Typesense — not a plug-and-play module with a marketing dashboard. But unlike them, understanding a reference's structure (diameter, length, material, vintage, ISBN) is included from indexing onward, via industry rule packs, rather than built query by query. And unlike Sensefuel or Doofinder, hosting stays in France, with simple pricing starting at €19/month, no sales negotiation.

A need for advanced visual merchandising (banners, personalized recommendations at scale) is still better covered by Sensefuel or Doofinder today. A need for generalist search over unstructured content remains Algolia's or Typesense's turf. Heurix's turf is the catalog where every reference carries precise information that customers rarely type the same way twice.

How to decide

One simple question before choosing: do your product references contain structured codes, dimensions, or identifiers (M8x20, DIN 933, ISBN, vintage) that your customers type in varying formats? If so, that's exactly the problem Heurix was built to solve — testable free for 14 days, no commitment.
